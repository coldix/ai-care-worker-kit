# Which free AI tool should I use?

*Last checked 22 Oct 2026. Free plans change often, so check each provider's own pages. Nothing here is paid for or sponsored.*

You don't need a paid plan for anything in this kit. Any of the tools below can turn your notes into an invoice, write a polite email or tidy a timesheet. Free plans limit how much you can use them in a day, and the limits move around, so if one tool says you've hit your limit, just paste the same prompt into another.

Whatever you choose, never paste a client's Medicare number, date of birth, health details or full address. Use initials or "Client A". And turn off model training where you can, using the setting named below.

## The tools

**ChatGPT** (OpenAI) is the all-rounder and the easiest place to start. Good for invoices, emails and tables, and it can read a photo of your handwritten notes. Free use is limited and ads may appear on the free plan in some countries. To stop your chats being used for training, go to Settings, Data controls, and turn off "Improve the model for everyone", or use a Temporary Chat ([OpenAI: data controls](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-how-can-i-delete-my-data-from-chatgpt)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

**Google Gemini** suits you if you already use Gmail and Google Sheets, and it's good at building tables you can paste into a spreadsheet. Your chats are kept and may be reviewed by people unless you turn off "Keep Activity" under Settings and help, Activity ([Google: Gemini Apps privacy](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

**Claude** (Anthropic) writes the most natural, polite emails of the group, which helps with chasing payments and replying to providers. The free plan has a daily message limit. Turn off "Help improve our AI models" under Settings, Privacy ([Anthropic: privacy settings](https://privacy.anthropic.com/)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

**Microsoft Copilot** is handy if you use Outlook, Word or Excel. If you're signed in, turn off "Training on conversation activity" and "Training on voice conversations" under your profile, Privacy ([Microsoft: Copilot privacy controls](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/microsoft-copilot-privacy-controls)). Copilot inside Word, Excel and Outlook on a Microsoft 365 Personal or Family plan isn't used for training. Apps for iPhone, Android and Windows.

**Meta AI** lives inside WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram, so it's always on your phone. It's fine for quick wording help, but Meta uses chats to improve its AI and offers no setting to stop that, so keep it to general questions with no client details at all ([Meta: privacy and generative AI](https://www.facebook.com/privacy/genai/)).

**Grok** (xAI) is quick and good at sums, such as totalling hours. Turn off "Improve the model" under Settings, Data controls ([xAI: consumer FAQ](https://x.ai/legal/faq)). Apps for iPhone and Android, and inside X.

**Le Chat** (Mistral) is a capable European option. Turn off "Allow your interactions to be used to train our models" in its settings ([Mistral: help centre](https://help.mistral.ai/)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

**DeepSeek** is free and strong at maths and step-by-step checking, but your data is stored in China under Chinese law, so don't put anything about clients into it ([DeepSeek: privacy policy](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

**Perplexity** is best for looking things up, because it shows its sources. Use it to check a rule, then click through to the official page. Turn off "AI data retention" in account settings ([Perplexity: privacy](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/legal/privacy-policy)). Apps for iPhone and Android.

## Which one for which job

For invoices and timesheets, ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. For polite emails and provider replies, Claude or ChatGPT. For checking a rule, Perplexity, then the official site it links to. For quick sums on the go, whichever app is already on your phone.

Remember that AI tools get facts and figures wrong. Check every total yourself, and check any rule against the official source before you rely on it.
