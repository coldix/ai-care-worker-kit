# Getting approved by a provider

*Last checked 22 Oct 2026. General information only.*

When a client who self-manages wants you, their provider has to approve you before it can pay you from the client's budget. The provider stays responsible for meeting the rules even when a third-party worker does the job, so it will check you properly ([health.gov.au: self-management](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/support-at-home/delivering-services-for-support-at-home/self-management-by-support-at-home-participants)). Providers can add an overhead of up to 10% of your price for managing the arrangement, so your rate and the amount charged to the budget may differ.

## What providers usually ask for

Expect to send your ABN, a current public liability certificate, your police certificate or NDIS Worker Screening Clearance ([health.gov.au: screening](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/aged-care-workforce/screening-requirements)), your rate and the services you offer, your bank details, and sometimes a signed supplier agreement and proof of relevant training or qualifications.

## Making it quick

Keep a "provider pack" folder with all of those documents as PDFs, named clearly with their expiry dates, such as "Public liability, expires 2027-03-31.pdf". When a new client asks, you can reply the same day. Ask the provider for their supplier form, their invoice guide and who to contact about payments, all in your first email.

## How the client fits in

The client (or their family) tells the provider they want you, agrees the service and the price, and the provider updates their care plan. You can't approve yourself, so stay friendly and patient with both sides. The [reply to a provider prompt](../prompts/reply-to-provider.md) helps with the back-and-forth.

More detail is in the CarePlan guide [Getting approved as an independent worker](https://careplan.au/guides/getting-approved-by-a-provider/).
