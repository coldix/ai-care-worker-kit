# Getting set up as a sole trader

*Last checked 22 Oct 2026. General information only, not tax or legal advice.*

Most independent care workers start as a sole trader. It's the simplest and cheapest way to run a business: you are the business, you use your own tax file number, and your business income goes on your normal tax return ([ATO: business structures](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/starting-registering-or-closing-a-business/starting-your-own-business/business-structures-key-tax-obligations)).

## Your ABN

An Australian Business Number is an 11-digit number that identifies your business. Providers will ask for it, and it goes on every invoice. It's free to apply through the Australian Business Register, and if your details are complete you usually get it straight away ([business.gov.au: register for an ABN](https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-for-an-australian-business-number-abn)). Only apply once you are actually running a business, because the ABR can ask you to prove it ([ABR: applying for an ABN](https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn)).

## A business name, if you want one

If you trade under your own first name and surname, such as "Jo Citizen", you don't need to register a business name. If you add anything, such as "Jo Citizen Cleaning", you must register it with ASIC, and there's a fee. Your registered name and ABN must then appear on your invoices ([ASIC: register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name)).

## Insurance

Public liability insurance covers you if someone is hurt or their property is damaged because of your work. It isn't compulsory by law for a sole trader with no staff, but almost every provider will ask for a current certificate before they pay you, so treat it as essential ([business.gov.au: insurance](https://business.gov.au/risk-management/insurance)). Personal accident or income protection cover is worth thinking about too, because if you're hurt, nobody else pays you.

## Worker screening

Anyone delivering funded aged care through a registered provider, including workers engaged by a client who self-manages, needs either a police certificate less than 3 years old or an NDIS Worker Screening Clearance. The provider checks it and keeps a record, so have a copy ready ([health.gov.au: screening requirements](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/aged-care-workforce/screening-requirements)). Some providers also ask for a first aid certificate or proof of vaccinations, depending on the work.

## GST

You must register for GST once your GST turnover reaches $75,000 a year, or when you expect it to. Below that it's optional, and if you choose to register you generally have to stay registered for at least 12 months ([ATO: registering for GST](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst/)). Watch your total each month: once you reach the threshold you have 21 days to register. Most aged care services are GST-free in any case, but registering changes your paperwork, so ask the ATO or a tax agent first.

## Super

As a sole trader you don't have to pay super guarantee for yourself, but you can choose to make personal contributions and may be able to claim a deduction for them ([ATO: business structures](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/starting-registering-or-closing-a-business/starting-your-own-business/business-structures-key-tax-obligations)). It's easy to forget for years, so set up a small regular transfer if you can.

## Setting tax aside

Nobody takes tax out of your payments, so the bill arrives later. A simple habit is to move a fixed share of every payment into a separate savings account the day it lands. How much depends on your total income, so check the [ATO tax rates](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents) or ask a tax agent. Once your business income grows, the ATO may put you on PAYG instalments, which spreads the bill across the year. The [tax set-aside prompt](../prompts/tax-set-aside.md) helps you work out a rough figure.

## Keeping records

You must keep records of your income and expenses for 5 years, in English, stored so they can't be changed or lost ([ATO: record keeping rules](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business)). For most care workers that means a copy of every invoice, every remittance from a provider, your timesheets, and receipts for things like insurance, cleaning products and your phone. A folder per financial year in Google Drive or OneDrive is enough.

## Contractor or employee?

If a provider controls how, when and where you work, you may legally be an employee, not a contractor. The Fair Work Ombudsman explains the difference ([Fair Work: independent contractors](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/independent-contractors)).
