# Invoicing a Support at Home provider

*Last checked 22 Oct 2026. General information only.*

When your client is on Support at Home, you usually invoice their provider, not the client. The provider checks the invoice, claims it from the client's budget through Services Australia, and pays you ([health.gov.au: how Support at Home works](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/support-at-home/about/how-the-support-at-home-program-works)). Every provider has its own small preferences, so ask for their invoice guide when you are approved. What follows works for almost all of them.

## What goes on the invoice

Start with your details: your name or registered business name, your ABN, phone and email. Your business name and ABN must be on invoices ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name)).

Then give it a unique invoice number. Use a simple sequence you never repeat, such as JC-1001, JC-1002, and an invoice date.

Next, who it's for: the provider's name, and the client's name and their client or reference number with that provider. The provider needs these to match the claim to the right budget. This is the one place a client's name belongs, and only on the copy you send the provider.

For each visit, one line with the date of service, the official service type (such as "Domestic assistance" or "Social support"), a short description of what you did, the hours, your hourly rate and the line total. The official names are on the [Support at Home service list](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/support-at-home/delivering-services-for-support-at-home/services-under-support-at-home).

Finish with the total amount due, payment terms (for example, 14 days), and your bank details: account name, BSB and account number.

## GST wording

Only a business registered for GST can issue a "tax invoice". If you're not registered, call it "Invoice" and add a line such as "No GST has been charged. Not registered for GST." ([business.gov.au: how to invoice](https://business.gov.au/finance/payments-and-invoicing/how-to-invoice)).

## Things that get invoices sent back

Vague descriptions like "services rendered", travel to and from the client on its own line unless the provider has agreed it, more hours than the client's service agreement allows, totals that don't add up, an ABN that doesn't match ABN Lookup, or an insurance certificate that has expired. Sometimes the client's quarterly budget has simply run out, which is not your fault, but it's worth asking the provider early.
