# Chasing late payments politely

*Last checked 22 Oct 2026. General information only.*

Most late payments are not deliberate. An invoice is stuck in a queue, a detail doesn't match, or the client's budget is short. A calm, specific reminder fixes most of them.

## A simple routine

On the day after the due date, send a short friendly reminder with the invoice number, date and amount, and attach the invoice again. A week later, ask whether anything is wrong with the invoice and who is looking after it. After that, phone the provider's accounts team and note who you spoke to and what they said. Keep it polite every time: the person reading your email probably didn't cause the delay.

## Check your side first

Before you chase, check the invoice against the [invoicing checklist](invoicing-a-provider.md). A wrong client reference or missing service type is the most common reason payment stalls.

## If it still isn't paid

If you have a written agreement, check what it says about payment terms. Small businesses can get free help from the Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman ([ASBFEO: dispute support](https://www.asbfeo.gov.au/disputes-assistance/dispute-support)). If the problem is with how a provider runs a client's care, the client can complain to the provider and then to the [Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission](https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/make-complaint).

The [overdue reminder prompt](../prompts/overdue-reminder.md) writes the emails for you.
